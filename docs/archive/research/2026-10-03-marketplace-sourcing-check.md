## The comparison OSE must beat

[Reolink RLK12-800WB4](https://store.reolink.com/us/4-camera-security-system/)
was displayed at **$659.99 on Oct 3**, only $39.01 below the $699 OSE checkpoint.
Its [exact bundle page](https://reolink.com/us/product/rlk12-800wb4/) identifies
four B800W 8 MP dual-band Wi-Fi cameras, RLN12W recorder, 2 TB HDD, four
camera power adapters, 24/7 local recording without Internet, two-way audio,
and a two-year warranty. Initial camera setup requires temporary Ethernet to
the NVR. It is a stronger **wire-free-data / local NVR** comparison than an old
PoE kit, though Reolink's closed ecosystem differs from OSE's open/replaceable
goal. Buyer sees a finished system; OSE has no qualified four-camera package
and unresolved live-view/saved-event reliability. Do not claim OSE is currently
comparable in experience or value.

The low-price floor is also real but not like-for-like:
[ANNKE AU-DW81KD1-V7-58BH-V2-P](https://www.annke.com/products/annke-3k-lite-security-camera-system)
displays **$254.99** for eight 2 MP analog/BNC cameras, DVR, and 1 TB HDD
(Oct 3). It is not eight IP/Wi-Fi cameras. Camera-count or headline-price
comparisons alone would mislead.

For a BYOC cost sanity check, the Tapo four-pack plus the Beelink direct price
already totals **$504.96 before** adequate recording storage, shipping/tax,
returns, support, warranty reserve, or OSE margin. This is arithmetic using
two current direct prices, **not** an OSE costed package. At 2–4 Mbps per
camera, four continuously recorded streams need roughly 0.6–1.2 TB for seven
days before filesystem/retention headroom; this is an estimate, not measured
Tapo output.

## Automation cost and next boundary

The worker keeps raw HTML/browser work off the daily computer and returns small
structured results. One four-page exact-URL check returned about **400 output
tokens**; browser launches took roughly **8 seconds/page** in this run. That
does **not** measure billed model tokens. The costly part is broad discovery,
unfiltered web-page output, and reasoning through incompatible variants. As a
planning estimate, a compact 10-item batch should use hundreds to a few
thousand output/context tokens, while a sourced comparison with cross-checks
can run into **many thousands to tens of thousands**. No exact token/billing
telemetry was available. Moving Chromium to `gm` saves local RAM/CPU, **not**
model tokens. Batch exact URLs, return only status/identity/price/seller/stock,
and stop after one block; avoid repeated browser retries and full HTML dumps.

**Human boundary:** public Alibaba search currently returns a verification
page, but the worker can inspect an exact URL. If you find an exact candidate
in your logged-in Alibaba browser, send only its product URL; the worker can
check title, supplier, MOQ, and tiers without account credentials. It cannot
prove shipping/duty, exact firmware, or a production quote. Do not share login
credentials; no purchase or supplier contact is requested. Until those gaps
are closed, the safest software path is compatibility testing with a
customer-owned camera and proving saved-video/live-view reliability, not
buying inventory.
